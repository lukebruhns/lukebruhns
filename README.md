# Luke Bruhns

Independent AI alignment researcher in Detroit, MI. I study how large language models represent and report moral values, how safety training shapes what they will and will not say about morality, and whether alignment can draw on a wider range of moral traditions than it does today.

By day I am Director of Cybersecurity at Open Dealer Exchange, where I lead governance, risk, and compliance and run the company's AI Governance Committee.

## Research interests

- Moral evaluation of LLMs (psychometric instruments, measurement validity)
- Refusal behavior and how it distorts measurement
- Value pluralism in alignment
- Constitutional and faith-based alignment, including Catholic moral thought

I am Catholic, and that tradition informs the questions I ask. The methods I build are meant to work for any tradition.

## Research

**Faith-based AI alignment** ([repo](https://github.com/lukebruhns/faith-based-ai-alignment))
Pre-registered evaluation ([OSF](https://osf.io/52xa7)) of 27 language models. Measures how a constitutional system prompt derived from the Catechism of the Catholic Church shifts model values on MFQ-2, the Christian Moral Values Scale, and MoReBench-Theory, and what it costs in general capability on MMLU. Paper in preparation.

**The Identity-Refusal Effect** ([repo](https://github.com/lukebruhns/identity-refusal-effect), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lukebruhns/identity-refusal-mfq2), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19618747))
Preprint. Safety-trained LLMs refuse first-person moral self-report at different rates across moral foundations, which skews MFQ-2 scores. 43,200 responses from 20 models, with a depersonalized item set that reduces the effect.
